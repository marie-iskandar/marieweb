### events

i was on a long, increasingly foggy trail, headed toward "tennessee." "tennessee" was only accessible by foot at the end of a long trail nobody had seen the end of. the trail attracted a lot of hikers from all walks of life, many of whom didn't quite seem to be sure why they were there. i had conversations with some of them, who talked about who they were, what they did, and what they planned to do when they finally got to "tennessee." the trail was sandwiched between a beautiful gentle river and a dark wild forest. the scenery was beautiful, but fairly repetitive -- there were footbridges across the river that led to bustling sunlit plazas, and a bunch of little houses dotted the forest. they seemed pristine, but abandoned. if you looked in the windows, most had very personalized contents -- a huge ornate dollhouse in one, a huge collection of books and art supplies in another, seemingly no longer in use. on the other side of the trail, across the river, it looked beautiful at first glance, but if you paused for a moment, you could tell that the people there looked like shadows, and didn't seem able to go anywhere.

after some time on the trail i ran into my great-grandma, who passed away some time ago. she offered to let me stay in her "old house", which was in the forest. she warned me that she had "lost the key", so i'd be able to get into her house, but not back out. the house didn't look like hers, and she didn't seem willing to enter it herself, so i began to doubt whether it was really her, and declined her offer.

i opted to keep walking the trail instead, but eventually it got too foggy to see and i got too tired to proceed. i passed out where i stood. instead of lilacs or lavender, i awoke to the smell of my own rancid sweat.

### interpretation

both sides of the trail seemed like a kind of early death. the plaza across the river felt like abandonment of the self. the forest felt like clinging to it to the exclusion of others. both seemed permanent and separated from the true ending. i'm sure it was influenced by Familial Lore:
some members of my family have reported having the same dream when their lives are in danger.
they see a footbridge over a quiet river going toward a beautiful garden. they naturally want to cross, but on the way,
are stopped by someone -- variously "a monk", a dead loved one, or someone named "lily". they would be told that they
were "allowed" to cross, but encouraged to go back until it was "really their time."

people who have stated they had this dream: grandpa, great grandma, grandma, my mom, and my aunt. grandpa got the monk
and "lily," mom got "lily" and grandpa, everyone else has just gotten grandpa. sometimes they awaken to the smell of
lilacs or lavender.

### open questions / comments

* why was i dressed like superman for part of the dream?
* why did the dream insist that "grandma"'s house was located on "cigarette street"? (there were no streets in the forest)
* why "tennessee"? i have no personal or familial links to tennessee and i do not believe it is only accessible by foot
* is "lily" lilith or something? apparently grandpa advised against thinking too hard about who she was other than to say that she was someone who "protects the family". i don't know anything else about her, but according to mom, she apparently *hates* the "[lesser key of solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lesser_Key_of_Solomon)" and once reacted violently to prevent my mom from bringing a copy of it into the house
* one of my last conversations with great-grandma irl was pretty scary. she did not have dementia. she asked if i loved my mom. i said yes. she smiled, and said, "no, you don’t. you can’t really love, can you?" it was so out-of-character, but i still wonder sometimes whether she was right.

yours, marie, who has never been to tennessee or worn a superman costume
